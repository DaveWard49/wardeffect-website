🏥 The Superbug Problem: Delaminated Surfaces

Hospitals face an even more insidious challenge: delaminated surfaces where paint, coatings, or epoxy flooring separates from the substrate, creating hidden voids perfect for pathogen colonies.

🎨 Delaminated Paint

Hospital walls are painted repeatedly over decades. Old paint layers separate from walls, creating microscopic gaps where MRSA, C. difficile, and other superbugs colonize—completely protected from surface disinfection.

🏢 Epoxy Floor Separation

Operating rooms use epoxy flooring for easy cleaning. Over time, the epoxy delaminates from concrete underneath. Bacteria colonize the void between layers and emerge through microscopic cracks during surgery.

🚿 Tile and Grout Voids

Bathroom tiles separate from backing. Shower walls develop gaps behind seemingly intact surfaces. Moisture penetrates, pathogens colonize, and no amount of bleach reaches the reservoir.

⚙️ Equipment Coating Failure

Stainless steel equipment with protective coatings. Coatings delaminate from thermal cycling. Legionella and Pseudomonas thrive in the hidden interface—invisible to visual inspection.

Why Superbugs Win:

Delaminated surfaces can harbor millions of bacteria in a single square centimeter. They emerge in mass regardless of surface cleaning. Traditional inspection cannot detect delamination until it's severe. By then, the pathogen reservoir is massive.
