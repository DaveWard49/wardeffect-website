Patents & Protection

Ward Effect Technology is protected by a portfolio of granted patents across five jurisdictions, with additional applications under examination. The IP covers the core Ward Effect principle and its applications across sensing, purification, and structural testing.

Granted Patents

Confirmed Intellectual Property

NZ 739314 — Granted
Electromagnetic Acoustic Sensing System
New Zealand Granted PCT/NZ2019/050002

Foundation patent establishing the core Ward Effect principle — deliberate engineering of stable parasitic oscillation in a feedback circuit where the medium being measured is physically inside the loop. The oscillation fingerprint changes measurably based on medium condition, enabling nano-resolution measurement without laboratory equipment.

Key Claims

  • Parasitic oscillation feedback loop
  • Medium-inside-circuit architecture
  • Non-intrusive volume measurement
  • Self-calibrating sensing
NZ 770993 — Granted
Acoustic Sensing and Water Purification System
New Zealand Granted

Extends Ward Effect to water purification applications. Covers the use of controlled cavitation and acoustic sensing for chemical-free pathogen destruction, chlorine dosing detection, and water quality monitoring. The foundation of the water purification bucket mission.

Key Claims

  • Chemical-free water purification
  • Cavitation pathogen destruction
  • Chlorine dosing detection
  • Water quality monitoring
NZ 778527 — Granted
Library-Configured Acoustic Sensing and Actuation System
New Zealand Granted

Covers the LEF (Library Entry Format) sparse library system enabling configurable multi-application sensing from a single hardware platform. The three-tier retrieval architecture allows the same Ward Effect node to serve pipeline leak detection, structural testing, water purification, and drain de-silting applications through library configuration alone.

Key Claims

  • LEF sparse library system
  • Three-tier retrieval architecture
  • Multi-application single platform
  • Library-configured switching
